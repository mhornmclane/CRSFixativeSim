Accounting & assumptions
One cycle = one reverse stroke + one forward stroke. Counts exclude ocean priming and sample collection. All plumbing starts air-filled; the ocean and bag contain liquid. Ocean priming fills the main line and filter before the fixative sequence.
Before the final cycle: first reverse = L2 + L1 + reverse margin; later reverse = L1 + reverse margin. Forward strokes retain the forward margin in L1. The final intake is trimmed to meet the exact target and final buffer, without overfill. If needed, the penultimate forward stroke is shortened too. Reverse overpump margin applies only to earlier full loads.
Final buffer: positive = fixative remaining in L1; zero = trailing edge at T1; negative = trailing edge inside L3. Effective buffer is capped below by forward margin − L3, so at least the forward margin remains ahead of the filter inlet. This permission to enter L3 applies only at the end of the plan; another reverse stroke cannot remove fluid trapped in L3.
Delivered means fixative reaching the F1 inlet; filter internal hold-up is not modeled. Initial L3 seawater is purged before fixative is counted. Bag draw = delivered + ocean loss + fixative retained in L1, L2 and L3.
Ideal displacement: no mixing, gas compression, valve leakage or pump calibration error. Include connector and valve volumes in the appropriate segment. Layout is schematic; fluid fill fractions follow the entered volumes. Priming timing is illustrative; the model does not validate a physical no-seawater guarantee.
